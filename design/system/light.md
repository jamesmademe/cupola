# The title light

Part of the brand book's *Colour* ([README](README.md#colour)): how a room takes its light from the artwork that leads it, and where the light stops. The tone rule and the room's surfaces stay in the README.

**Direction.** The light is derived from the artwork, never picked: a 64 px decode, quantised (Celebi, 128 colours) and scored off the UI isolate, dropping clusters darker than HCT tone 18 or lighter than 92 (the black ground and the lettering). From its hue: the **light** is HCT chroma 16–48 at tone 68; the tinted **action fill** chroma 12–36 at tone 82, under an ink of chroma 4–16 at tone 12 (about 10:1 on every title); the day room's tinted primary chroma 16–48 at tone 40 under white (6.4:1 or better). A poster glows with its light at `elevation.poster.glow` (0.8 · day 0.35), the hero at `elevation.hero.glow` (0.48 · day 0.35). **A room takes its colours from the one image that leads it** (light, glow, bar sheen, tinted primary, chart hue). A card about a title may carry that title's light inside its own edges (Coming up, the collection to complete, the downloads, the queue), at half strength, even in a room lit by another image: the card is the title's, the room is the page's. Many dark posters derive a blue light: that is the catalogue, not a defect.

**The light is a picture, not a hue.** The room is lit by the artwork itself, baked once from the same 64 px decode (`bakeRoomArt` in `lib/core/room_art.dart`, in the same `compute` as the colours, carried as `TitleLight.art`): cropped square, blurred (a sigma of 11% of its side, run three sigmas past the edge into transparency), saturated as CSS `saturate()` does (2 in the dark room, 1.4 on paper) and kept within what text can sit on: laid in at the top of the room, it is at most relative luminance 0.03 in the dark room (half the brightest room measured, so a bright poster's room is not five times brighter than a dark one's; a dark poster stays under it) and at least 0.72 on paper, so a white poster cannot glare and a black one tints paper instead of darkening it. The room lays it in a box 630 wide (or 0.64 of a wider room, then from its left), 60 above the top and 640 tall, at 0.72, under a scrim of the ground: 12% at the top, 50% at 300 (60% on paper), 94% at 540, whole at 640. On screen that is one texture scaled up: nothing blurs while a page scrolls. A new picture comes up over `motion.light` and the old one fades under it; Reduce Motion seats it at once. A light kept from an earlier launch has no picture until it is read again: the room is lit by its colour as a lamp meanwhile.

**The lamp.** A room with no artwork (infrastructure, settings, a download client's room) and a title whose artwork has too little chroma are lit by the neutral lamp, `color.title-light.fallback` `#8A8A99`: an ellipse hung at the top centre of the room, 40 down, 230 tall and 0.42 of the room (plus 160) wide up to 480, whole at its centre at `opacity.ambient-glow` (0.42 · day 0.14) and gone at its rim. The lamp never changes with state: a fault in a lamp room brings its own amber remedy.

**A service's room** is lit by what leads it: a library (Radarr, Sonarr, Lidarr) by the first download stuck in its queue, else the first moving, else what arrived last (Radarr, Sonarr); Seerr by what is trending; a media server (Jellyfin, Plex) by what its first session plays; Bazarr by the first title it wants subtitles for; a public picture only; otherwise by the lamp. Its tabs are one room (`RoomLightScope` in `ServiceHomeScreen`), so switching tab never changes the light, and the band and the tab bar frost that same room. A media server's room reads who is watching again every 15 s while its Overview or its playbacks tab is seen, and its figure, its Now playing and its light read that one answer, so they never disagree.

**A picture behind a key lends no light, so the arrs lend theirs.** A media server's artwork and the arrs' own copies are fetched with a key, and a light is read from a public picture only. A title a media server plays is named by the ids the server holds for it (`getTitleKey`: the movie's, or the series' for an episode) and lit by the remote poster (TMDB's, TheTVDB's) of the arr record holding those ids (`arrPublicPosterProvider`); a title Bazarr wants subtitles for is named by its arr's own id and taken only when that record's title is the one Bazarr gives (`arrPublicPosterByIdProvider`: the same id on another connection is another title). No arr holds it: the lamp.

**Of several pictures, the most coloured.** A room that may take its light from more than one title (Discover: the next thing out, then the Trending door's three) waits until each light is read, then takes the one whose picture lights the dark room with the most chroma (`RoomArt.chroma`, the mean of the brightest channel less the dimmest over the bake), the first on a tie: a dark next release no longer leaves the room grey while Trending holds colour.

**The floating chrome takes a sheen of the room in front.** The bar and the rail are lit enamel: the room's light at 14% over the enamel at their top, gone by 70% of their height, and the selected pill tinted by it (the light at 18% in the dark room, the title's day primary at 18% on paper), drifting to a new room's over `motion.light`. A lamp room casts no sheen: plain enamel and the strong fill. The room in front is the one whose branch the shell shows and whose page is its navigator's current one, the deepest of those (a tab's room inside its service's), the last to come in front on a tie; with none in front (a sheet over the page) the light is kept (`FrontRoomLight`, `RoomSheen`).

**Every veil paints the room.** What covers content scrolling under the clock (`StatusBarScrim`), a bar (`GlassSurface`) or a band paints the room itself (`paintRoom`, picture and lamp), faded by its own alpha, never the flat ground: at rest it cannot be seen, and a lit room keeps its light up to the top edge.

**Today** the Media page, a shelf (a person, a collection, an artist, an author), a download, the calendar, the Home (the slide in front, else the next thing out), Activity (the download that leads it), Discover (the most coloured of the next release and Trending's first three), the libraries' rooms, Seerr's, the media servers' and Bazarr's are lit by their picture; the Home's Coming up and Downloads, Discover's collection, Activity's downloads, the Coming up of Radarr's and Sonarr's Overviews (`ServiceOverviewSection.light`), Seerr's Needs a decision and a media server's sessions carry their first title's light, and their rows its poster (`ServiceOverviewArt`, the title's initials where there is none); a rail's posters (the Overviews' rails, Discover's and the Home's shelves, the Home's rows) glow with their own light once drawn (`PosterGlow`, `ContentCard.lit`, `MediaPosterCard`). Not yet: a grid's posters, which keep the lamp's glow (a light a poster is too many reads for a grid).
